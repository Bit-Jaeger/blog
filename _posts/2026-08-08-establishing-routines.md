## /what is a 'routine'?
After a quick search on the web I ended up with one thing that almost every definition has in common: doing something on a regular and always in a similar or same way. <br>
And I do think this is a broad but still pretty accurate way of defining a routine. 

## /my routines
For me it has always been a little bit harder because of me working nightshifts in between uni - so every week and every day was somewhat new. Thinking about fixing the sleeping schedule, when to wake up etc. <br>
But in the latest weeks I decided to switch jobs - get something with a regular timeslot in the day. And ever since my routines have been making my life quite a lot easier. <br>


## /how did I build my routines
To be honest the routine I probably like the most besides the regular bed and waking up times is my morning routine. <br> I didn't originally come up with one that is somewhat 'optimized' or follows any guide from which you may find dozens online. It just came a little bit more natural. I didn't even notice I had a routine until after it was already established deeply. <br>
But as i was quite stressed with studying because of my upcoming exams and one thing I always made sure was to take my time in the morning and start nicely into the day. I woke up, made myself ready in the bathroom and then proceeded to drink a coffee, make me a small breakfast of 2 fried eggs and ate that while watching the news of the day before. 

## /why I feel like routines are a good thing
And this is how I started my day with the same structure. After that I knew it was time to start being productive and even though I took my time with breakfast and everything around it. I did get going immediately afterwards. <br>
I felt like it was helping me quite a lot with procrastinating, having a structured start into every day while not needing to think about the next steps gave me some kind of mental wellbeing I did not expect from it. <br> 
It might sound a bit corny but having this did help me a lot - having a routine made myself focus more on the task ahead rather than having to plan my day as an additional task. <br>
This is what ultimately led me to the perception that routines just make you think about what you do a little less, which leads to having more time and energy on the real tasks ahead.

## /future routines
After my exams are all done as of yesterday I am for sure not changing the morning routine up. I will take the 'holidays' just as time for things that I want to come after - tinkering with an arduino, smaller side projects and in general becoming more competent in what I do. <br>
I am not quite sure what routines I want to establish but I sure do know that having them kind of lay out the structure of my days will help me get into 'focus mode' better and make me a more productive person. <br>

## /looking forward
I do think I am always trying to get a little bit better just in general as I was yesterday - I firmly believe that routines will take their part with this and will support me in this regard in the future.
<br>
I am curious on what the next few months will hold for me but I looking forward to find out and growing during the process!
<br>
On this regard - stay curious, go and explore and always try to be atleast 1% better than you were yesterday.
